SPORT REFERENCE: ULTIMATE

About the game
- Two teams of seven on a rectangular field with an endzone at each end. A point is scored by catching the disc in the attacking endzone.
- The player with the disc may not run with it. They pivot on one foot and have a limited time to throw, counted out loud by the marker (the stall count, up to 10).
- Possession changes on a turnover: a dropped or blocked pass, an interception, a throw out of bounds, or a stall-out.
- Ultimate is self-refereed. Players make and resolve their own calls (foul, travel, pick, contest). Spirit of the Game, meaning fair play, knowledge of the rules and respectful communication, is part of the sport and is coached like any other skill.
- Points start with a pull: a long throw from the defending team to the attacking team.

Terminology
- Terms vary between teams and regions; several names for the same thing are listed below. Use the term the coach used. When it is unclear which skill the coach means, ask instead of guessing.
- Common abbreviations: BH (backhand), FH (forehand), IO (inside-out), OI (outside-in), D (defence or a block).

Skill areas

1. Throwing
- Backhand: the most common throw, thrown across the body. Key points: grip, pivot step out to the side, release point, flat and stable flight.
- Forehand (flick): thrown from the side of the throwing arm, wrist-driven. Often the harder throw for newer players; a common goal area.
- Hammer: overhead throw that flies upside down and lands at a steep angle. Useful to get over a mark or a zone.
- Scoober: short overhead throw, flipped upside down, for short distances around a tight mark.
- Push pass: short, simple throw used by beginners or for short resets.
- Huck: a long throw downfield, usually to a deep cut.
- Inside-out (IO) and outside-in (OI): throws that curve; the disc's tilt (angle) at release decides the curve. IO starts on the inside and curves away; OI starts wide and curves back in.
- Break throws: throws to the side the mark is trying to take away (the break side). An around break goes around the mark's outside; an inside break goes between the mark and their body. Usually needs a pivot and a fake.
- Pivot and fakes: moving the non-pivot foot and faking a throw to open space past the mark.
- Throw qualities coaches talk about: height and speed of release, release point (high, low, wide), touch (soft, catchable) versus zip (fast), flight stability (wobble), and throwing in wind (upwind, downwind, crosswind).

2. Catching
- Pancake catch: two hands, one above and one below the disc, clapping it.
- Claw catch: one or two hands gripping the rim, for discs above the chest or below the knees.
- Watching the disc into the hands; catching at the highest point when contested.
- Layout: a diving catch or block (see Safety).
- Sky: a jumping catch over another player.

3. Cutting (attacking without the disc)
- Under cut: towards the thrower. Deep cut: away from the thrower towards the endzone.
- Timing: starting a cut when the thrower is ready to throw, not before.
- Changing direction sharply to get free from the defender.
- Clearing: leaving the space quickly after a cut that did not get the disc, so the next cutter has room.
- Continuation: after a catch, looking for the next throw quickly while the defence is still moving.

4. Handling (offence around the disc)
- Handlers: players who mostly throw and keep possession near the disc. Cutters: players who mostly move downfield.
- Dump: a short backwards or sideways pass to a handler to reset the stall count. Swing: a pass across the field to shift the play to the other side.
- Reset timing: offering a dump before the stall count gets high (often by stall 5 to 6).
- Give and go: pass, then cut immediately for the return pass.

5. Team offence structures
- Vertical stack: cutters lined up down the middle of the field, cutting out of the line.
- Horizontal stack (ho stack): cutters spread across the field.
- Side stack and iso plays: structures that give one cutter space.

6. Defence
- Marking: guarding the thrower, at least one disc diameter away, counting the stall out loud.
- Force: the side the mark pushes the thrower towards, taking the other side away. Called by throw (force forehand, force backhand), by field side (force home, force away, sideline), or straight up (no side).
- Person defence: each defender guards one attacker, usually positioned to stop the easy cut.
- Zone defence: defenders guard areas instead of people, for example a cup (three players around the thrower), wings and a deep. Often used in wind.
- Poach: leaving one's attacker to cover space or a likely throw.
- Blocks: hand block or foot block on the throw, and blocks on the disc in the air.

7. Field sense and decisions
- Seeing the open player, choosing safe versus risky throws, using the stall count, reading the force, knowing where space is.

8. Spirit of the Game
- Knowing the main rules, making calls calmly, discussing calls respectfully, and taking part in the spirit circle after games.

How skills can be checked
Examples for making goals checkable. Choose distances and numbers to fit the player's level; these are starting points, not standards.
- Throws without a mark: completed throws out of 10 to a partner at a set distance (for example 10, 15 or 20 metres), counted in a warm-up or drill.
- Break throws: completed break throws out of 10 past a static mark, later past an active mark.
- Hucks: throws out of 10 that land in a marked target area at a set distance.
- Throws in wind: completed throws out of 10 in a set wind direction.
- Catching: drops per drill or per session, counted by a teammate.
- Marking: in a three-person break-mark drill, throws the mark lets through to the forced-away side out of 10.
- Cutting and clearing: in a scrimmage, whether the player clears the lane after a cut that does not get the disc, as observed by the coach or a teammate on a set number of points.
- Resets: in scrimmage, whether the player offers or throws the dump before stall 6 when there is no upfield option.
- Practice between sessions: times per week the player practises a throw on their own or with a partner (wall, partner throwing, set number of throws).
- Spirit: whether the player can explain the main calls, or leads part of a spirit circle.

Progressions
- From no mark to a static mark to an active mark.
- From short to longer distances, and from no wind to wind.
- From drills to small games (for example 3 against 3 on a small field) to full scrimmages.
- Constraints that bring out a skill in games: forehand only, lower stall count (for example stall 6) to speed up resets, smaller field to practise breaks, scoring only in a marked area.
- Common drills (names vary): go-to drill (cutting and throwing to an under cut), box drill (four corners), break-mark drill (thrower, mark and receiver), huck drill.

Safety
- Layouts and skies carry a risk of collisions and falls. Practise layouts only on soft grass with a progression, and remind players to watch out for other players in the air.
- Dangerous play, such as running through a player to reach the disc, is against the rules and the spirit of the game.
- Do not give medical advice on injuries. Refer to a doctor or physiotherapist.
